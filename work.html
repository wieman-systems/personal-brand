↗

A hands-on lesson, placed right in front of you.

Original · collaborative hackathon project

Original · collaborative hackathon project / 2026

GhostHands

GhostHands brings demonstrations out of a flat video and onto a real tabletop. Translucent hands, animated models, spoken instructions, and reference video share one spatial workspace on Apple Vision Pro.

What I built

  • A table-anchored workspace with animated paper-boat instructions and hand-joint recording and replay.
  • Interactive teaching models for engines, turbines, gears, brakes, pumps, cams, belts, and crank mechanisms.
  • Lesson authoring with written steps, video timestamps, fingertip-placed arrows, and importable lesson files.
  • Voice commands, offline narration, and an experimental iPhone-to-Apple Watch haptic relay.

The idea behind it

Watching someone do a task is different from understanding where your own hands should go. GhostHands explores that gap by putting the demonstration where the work happens.

My contribution

Built with Ethan Holm at SASEHacks. My work centered on the native spatial workspace, interaction, learning tools, mechanical labs, and companion-device experiments.

PROJECT CONTEXT

Submitted to SASEHacks. The images are simulator captures. Demonstrations are authored illustrations; arbitrary YouTube videos are not automatically converted to 3D. Watch feedback remains experimental.

Independent visionOS adaptation

Independent visionOS adaptation / 2026

Crank

A native Apple Vision Pro adaptation of Crank It! that turns the game's machinery into a spatial interaction problem: a lift, physical controls, a CRT console, and the tension of working in a confined industrial space.

What I built

  • A themed three-dimensional CRT menu and equipment console, designed for mixed reality.
  • Physical, look-and-pinch, and hybrid controls with adjustable interaction settings.
  • Placement and seated/reclined calibration, spatial audio, and revised pause and return flows.
  • Iterations on the lift, crank, keyboard interactions, cables, collision, lighting, and lifecycle recovery.

The idea behind it

A port becomes interesting when the original interaction is rethought for the device. Here, the challenge was making machinery feel usable with bare hands while preserving the game's atmosphere.

My contribution

Native adaptation, spatial interface, control design, and integration work. Original Crank It! by James Dornan and Mike Klubnika; original game content retains its creators’ authorship.

PROJECT CONTEXT

Personal experimental adaptation, not an official release. Headset comfort and final interaction quality remain iterative. No game download is distributed here.

Wieman OS

Collaborative academic prototype

Collaborative academic prototype / 2026

POLY

POLY explores spatial learning through connected code, anatomy, and other systems. Instead of flattening every relationship into a document, it gives the learner a map they can arrange, inspect, and follow.

What I built

  • A web study workspace and a native Vision Pro prototype with connected, movable source panes.
  • Code imports, dependency tracing, expandable readers, and grounded explanations.
  • Surround layouts, panel grips, resizing, and experiments with room-aware placement.
  • Learning flows for exploring, tracing, comparing, explaining, and pinning evidence.

The idea behind it

Some ideas are easier to understand when their relationships stay visible. POLY asks what learning looks like when the room becomes the workspace.

My contribution

Technical prototype and native Vision Pro development within a five-person OU project team.

PROJECT CONTEXT

Academic prototype. The submitted explanation engine used deterministic logic; it should not be mistaken for a general-purpose live AI tutor. Room placement and gesture comfort remain areas of iteration.

My contribution

PROJECT CONTEXT

Archived prototype using sample cases and scripted intake. The calculation engine is implemented; some analytical assumptions remained open for further review.
